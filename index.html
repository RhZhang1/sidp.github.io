Overview

The final paper contrasts classic imitation diffusion with SIDP's reward-guided self-imitation and shows the resulting real-world deployment on two mobile platforms.

Abstract

SIDP replaces demonstration-driven diffusion training with reward-guided self-imitation.

Diffusion policies can represent diverse trajectories, but standard imitation learning often carries suboptimal demonstrations into training and forces inference into a computationally expensive generate-then-filter pipeline. SIDP instead samples from its own policy, scores candidate trajectories with environment reward, and updates the denoising model with a reward-weighted objective.

What changes

  • Self-imitation concentrates the trajectory distribution and removes the need for auxiliary selectors.
  • Goal-agnostic exploration preserves multimodality, while a reward-driven curriculum stabilizes cold-start training.
  • The final system reports 79.11 mSR / 72.72 mSPL / 83.35 mRSR on InternVLA-N1 S1, 110 ms inference on Jetson Orin Nano, and 90.25 mSR in real-world tests.

Method

The policy samples candidate trajectories, keeps the top-ranked set by reward, and fits a weighted denoising loss.

Weighted update

The reward weights use a temperature-controlled softmax:

$$w(a_t^{(i)}) = \frac{\exp(r(s_t, a_t^{(i)})/\tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^{k} \exp(r(s_t, a_t^{(j)})/\tau)}$$ $$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SIDP}} = \sum_{i=1}^{k} w(a_t^{(i)})\, \mathbb{E}_{t_{dn}, \epsilon} \Big[\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(a_t^{(i), t_{dn}}, t_{dn}, s_t)\|_2^2\Big]$$
  • The paper finds \(\tau = 1.0\) to be the best balance between sharp selection and stable convergence.
  • Goal-agnostic exploration keeps the policy multi-modal instead of collapsing onto a single path.
  • Reward-driven curriculum learning filters unlearnable scenes early and reduces cold-start instability.
